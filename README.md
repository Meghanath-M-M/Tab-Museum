### Project Description
It is museum of all tabs opened in chrome, Museum of Tabs turns your embarrassing browser history — abandoned tabs, half-read articles, forgotten clipboard text — into a pretentious art gallery exhibit, complete with gold frames and snobbish curator captions rating each one's "artistic value" and "significance." 

### The Problem (that doesn't exist)
You have 47 tabs open, a clipboard full of forgotten copy-pastes, and a "read later" article from 2024. None of it is actually a problem — but doesn't it feel like it deserves a reckoning anyway?
Museum of Tabs treats your digital clutter as a body of work: gold frames, velvet ropes, and a pretentious curator who calls your abandoned recipe tab "a haunting meditation on intent versus execution."

### The Solution (that nobody asked for)
Museum of Tabs — an AI curator that turns your open tabs and clipboard history into gallery exhibits, complete with gold frames and pretentious artistic critiques nobody requested.

# Installation

To install this extension locally in your Chrome browser:

Unzip this folder to a location on your computer.
Open Google Chrome and type chrome://extensions/ into the URL bar.
In the top right corner, toggle on Developer mode.
Click the Load unpacked button in the top left.
Select the folder you just unzipped (the one containing this README.md file).

# Run
 Setup Guide
1. Get a Free Gemini API Key
The AI Curator requires a Gemini API key to write its hilarious backstories for your tabs.

Go to Google AI Studio and sign in with your Google account.
Click the "Create API key" button in the top left.
Copy the generated key.
2. Configure the Extension
Pin the Extension: Click the puzzle piece icon 🧩 in Chrome's top right toolbar and "pin" the Museum of Tabs icon so it's easily accessible.
Add your API Key: Click the museum icon to open it. It will immediately prompt you for your key. Paste the key you copied and hit Enter.
3. Usage & Features
Explore: Scroll horizontally to walk through your open tabs and clipboard fragments.
Trigger an Auction: Close a random tab in your browser, then open the museum and scroll to the final room to see it marked as "Sold at Auction"!


### Project Documentation
For Software:
Museum of Tabs — Short Documentation
What it is: A Chrome extension that turns your open tabs and clipboard history into an art gallery, with an AI curator writing pretentious critiques of each one.
The Problem (that doesn't exist): Your 47 open tabs and forgotten clipboard text aren't a real problem — but they feel like they deserve a reckoning.
The Solution (nobody asked for): Every tab becomes a framed "exhibit" with a fake title, medium, dramatic AI-written critique, and arbitrary "Artistic Value" score.
Tech Stack:
Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS — no frameworks
Chrome Extension APIs (chrome.tabs, chrome.storage)
Gemini API (via raw fetch(), no SDK)
Google Fonts (Playfair Display, Roboto)
Why it's useless (on purpose): Museum-level effort applied to something with zero real stakes — that mismatch is the whole joke.
